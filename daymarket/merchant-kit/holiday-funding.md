## The honest funding model — RESOLVED Oct 4 (his call)

His standing method (Oct 4): the shop's own holidays are THEIRS, not ours. Our profit is memberships + fees + standards. The resolution, in his words:

- **The Daybreak Drop is dual-funded (both true):** $10.99 is Ellianna's promotional holiday price — their call, their margin — AND DayMarket's 15% commission applies to the transaction ($1.65/dozen to us). The $4 discount vs $14.99 is real because $14.99 is the true in-store price.
- **Rule for every future holiday:** the business sets the promo price (their margin call) + our cut applies on top.
- **Note:** 15% is Ellianna's specific deal. Other merchants are profile fee + membership unless negotiated.

What remains is the page framing: the Drop should read as Ellianna's holiday price presented to members, not as "our member discount" with no named funder. The profile already notes true in-store prices — extend that honesty to who pays for the discount.

## Commission nuance

The 15% commission is on donut sales he brings in. Discounted claims shrink it:

- Daybreak Drop: 15% of $10.99 = $1.65/dozen vs 15% of $14.99 = $2.25. That is $0.60 less per claim, $15.00 less across all 25 claims.
- Fritter Friday: the dozen still sells at full price, so commission is unaffected. The fritter cost sits with the shop.
- First Hunt: the prize dozen is a cost to whoever funds it — $22.99. Name that funder too.

## Why not DayMarket-funded

$197.69 of weekly holiday exposure ÷ $12/mo Holiday Pass ≈ **17 passes to fund one launch week** of holidays. With zero paying members today, DayMarket-funded discounts are not viable — a second reason the merchant-promo model is the honest one right now. Revisit only when pass revenue covers it with margin left over.
