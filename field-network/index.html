The gap

Why the public internet is insufficient.

AI systems learn from the public internet. But the public internet does not contain how work actually happens.

The knowledge that keeps organizations running lives in experienced people: the workarounds nobody documented, the judgment calls that prevent failures, the local context that changes every answer, the exceptions that prove the process incomplete. None of it is published. None of it is searchable. When AI is asked to help with real work, it guesses — because the truth was never written down.

Readable exists to close that gap at the source: capture operational truth where it lives, under permission, with a chain of evidence a human can inspect.

How an assignment works

Assignment lifecycle.

  1. Authorization. Nothing begins without written permission from the organization. Scope, locations, people, and information boundaries are agreed first.
  2. Scoping. One bounded workflow or question. What the system is supposed to do, what knowledge it depends on, where it could fail, who is affected.
  3. Capture. A trained team documents the work as it happens — structured interviews, observed practice, reviewed documents. Only what was authorized, nothing more.
  4. Human review. Captured material is reviewed by a named human before it becomes anything else. Errors, gaps, and sensitivities are caught here.
  5. Governed asset. What survives review becomes a documented knowledge asset with its evidence chain attached: source, authorization, date, reviewer.

Readable does not roam, scrape, record, or enter sites without authorization. Every assignment is invited work, or it does not happen.

Where the work happens

Territory model.

Capture is organized by territory — a city, a region, a sector — because operational truth is local. The same workflow runs differently in different places, and the differences are the knowledge.

Local teams

Each territory is served by trained people who know the place — its organizations, its conditions, its context.

Who does the work

Team standards.

Capture teams are trained and qualified before they touch a live assignment.

Trained

Team members complete capture standards and qualifications covering interview method, observation protocol, evidence handling, and confidentiality.

Attributable

Every piece of captured knowledge is attributable to a named team member and a named reviewer. No anonymous inputs, no unowned claims.

Bounded

Assignments carry explicit boundaries — what is in scope, what is off limits, when it ends. The team works inside the boundary or stops.

Human-reviewed

A human with authority reviews captured material before it becomes a governed asset. The machine never certifies its own work.

How truth gets captured

Capture methods.

Three methods, used in combination, always under authorization.

Structured interviews

Guided conversations with the people who do the work — capturing judgment, exceptions, and the reasons behind the process.

Observed practice

Documenting the workflow as it actually runs, including the workarounds the manual never mentions.

Reviewed documents

Existing records, reports, and procedures — read against what people say, with discrepancies flagged, not smoothed over.

Proof, not promises

Chain of custody.

Every governed knowledge asset carries its evidence chain: where it came from, who authorized the capture, when it happened, who reviewed it, and what changed along the way.

If a claim cannot be traced back to an authorized source and a human reviewer, it does not ship. That is the standard in every territory, on every assignment, without exception.

Travel and funding

Field work is funded work.

Capture teams go where the work is. Travel happens on explicit terms — agreed before the assignment, never assumed.

Deployments are funded through annual field partnerships: a 12-month commercial commitment, billed monthly, with scope confirmed in a scoping conversation. Travel terms are set per agreement — from local-flexible to client-funded — and no team travels until the commercial gate is satisfied.
