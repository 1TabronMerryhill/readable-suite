How it works

Six steps. Zero waiting on a human.

  1. You describe the problem. In your own words, through a guided interview with Read / co-pilot.
  2. Readable structures the evidence. Your answers become a structured packet: the workflow, where AI touches it, who decides, how outputs are checked, what happens when it fails.
  3. The readiness assessment. The packet is scored against the published rubric — eight dimensions, 0 to 2 each, same answers always produce the same score.
  4. Recommended intervention. The verdict prescribes the next move: a Sprint, the Academy, or go build — with the reasoning shown.
  5. Measured report. Delivered instantly: verdict, dimension scores with your answers cited, your 90-day measurement plan, methodology.
  6. Audit & calibration. Sampled human audits run behind the scenes to keep the rubric honest. They never gate your report — the money moves while everyone sleeps.

The rubric is public · v1.0

Here is exactly how you will be scored.

Derived from the Practical AI Readiness capstone objectives (Objectives 1–3: evaluate appropriateness, design the workflow, evaluate outputs against evidence). Each dimension scores 0 (absent), 1 (partial), or 2 (solid). Maximum 16.

D1 · Problem boundedness

Solid looks like: one workflow with a clear start and end.

D2 · Measurable outcome

Solid looks like: the intended outcome stated with a number or a date.

D3 · AI-task fit

Solid looks like: AI proposes; a human disposes. AI's role matches what AI does well.

D4 · Alternative weighed

Solid looks like: a non-AI (or simpler) approach was weighed and rejected for a reason.

D5 · Inputs & data boundaries

Solid looks like: authorized inputs are defined; sensitive data is excluded or governed.

D6 · Human authority

Solid looks like: a named human holds final decision authority.

D7 · Acceptance & verification

Solid looks like: written, checkable acceptance criteria; outputs verified against evidence.

D8 · Failure handling & output discipline

Solid looks like: a documented exception path; outputs are accepted, revised, rejected, or escalated — never auto-accepted.

Frequently asked questions.

Is this consulting?

No. It is a machine-scored diagnostic against a published rubric, built from the Practical AI Readiness capstone objectives. It tells you where you stand and what to do next — it does not replace working with a human on a complex engagement.

Who is it for?

Owner-led businesses and teams with one workflow they are considering (or already running) with AI — and who want an honest verdict before investing further.

What exactly do I receive?

A measured report: a verdict (Ready / Ready with safeguards / Not ready), all eight dimensions scored with your answers cited as evidence, a recommended intervention with reasoning, your structured workflow evidence back, and a 90-day measurement plan.

What if the verdict is “not ready”?

You get the gap list — which doubles as the brief for a Business Visibility Sprint ($2,500), the engagement that extracts and designs the workflow properly. The assessment fee does not apply to the Sprint; the assessment did the discovery the Sprint would have charged for.

Can the rubric really be deterministic?

The scoring is: fixed questions, fixed scoring criteria, fixed verdict bands. Two buyers with the same answers get the same score. What the rubric cannot do is verify that your answers describe reality — answer honestly and it will treat you honestly.

Do I have to talk to anyone?

No. That is the point. Pay, answer, receive the report. If your verdict prescribes a Sprint or the Academy, a human enters the picture then — on your terms.
